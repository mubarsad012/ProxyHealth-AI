2. What the app does

You take a photo of a letter, bill or notice. The app reads it and explains it in your language, in short cards. It shows the words on the page that each fact came from. It can point you to groups that a person checked.

ProxyHealth AI explains what a paper says. It does not give legal or medical advice, decide eligibility, or fill in forms.

There is no account and no login.

3. What is sent when you read a paper

When you read a paper, the photo goes to our server and then to Google's AI (Gemini). Our server does not keep it.

With each paper, the app sends:

  • the photos of the pages, and the parts you chose to hide. In the phone app: Parts you hide are blacked out on our server before Google's AI sees them. On the web: Parts you hide are blacked out on your device before anything is sent.
  • the language you want the answer in;
  • your state and the first 3 digits of your ZIP code, only if you typed them in Settings;
  • your choices for tone, date style and calendar notes;
  • the kind of phone or browser (for example "android" or "web");
  • the random device code (see section 7) and the version of the consent you accepted.

When you ask a question about a paper, the app sends the words of that paper, the answer cards, your last few questions and your new question. It does not send the photos again.

When you use "Stuck on a website?", the app sends the screenshot and what you are trying to do.

The app does not ask for your name, e-mail, phone number, contacts, exact location, full ZIP code or immigration status, and it never sends them on its own. Apart from what is printed on a paper, shown in a screenshot or typed in a question that you choose to send, none of these leave your device. It removes the hidden details stored inside photos (like where a photo was taken) before sending.

None of your own photos or papers are sent until you tap "I understand, continue". Before that, the app asks our server for its settings and for the words of the app in your language. If you type the name of a language, that name is sent.

If you tap "Try a sample paper first" or "Not now", you can still use the made-up sample papers. The app then asks our server for the list of samples. It sends which sample you picked, with the same settings as a real paper: your language, your state and ZIP prefix if you typed them, your choices and the kind of phone or browser. What you do with a sample is sent too: the questions you ask about it, what you type when you ask about a sample screen, your state and ZIP prefix when you ask for help nearby, your answers to "Was this clear?", and the sample's cards when you make a quick sheet. The sample paper is made up, but what you type is real: it is sent so it can be answered. On our paid service, a question you ask about a sample goes to Google's AI like any other question.

Our paid service: Google does not use your paper to improve its products. Google keeps a short safety log for a limited time, then deletes it.

Our sample service (for demos): Sample mode. No AI service is contacted. Your photo is only compared with our sample papers and is not kept.

Test builds that use a free AI key refuse real papers. They only work with the made-up sample papers that come with the app, and they refuse anything you type about a sample.

Test builds with a different AI. We also make test builds of the app for our own testing. They are not the app in the app stores. We use them with the made-up sample papers, with our own papers, or with papers from people who said yes. A test build may use an AI that is not Google's. Its consent screen names that AI before you send a paper:

  • AI on our server: an open AI model runs on the same computer as our server and reads the photo there. No other company's AI sees the paper.
  • An outside AI: the photo goes to our server and then to the company named on the consent screen. That company says it will not use the paper to train its AI. It may still keep a copy. We use this kind of test build only while we build the app, and only with our own papers.
  • Sample papers only: some test builds use their AI only with the made-up sample papers. They refuse real papers and anything you type about a sample.

4. Google Gemini

The AI that reads your paper is Google's Gemini API. We use it on Google's paid terms. (Test builds can use a different AI; see section 3.)

Google does not use your paper to improve its products. Google keeps a short safety log for a limited time, then deletes it.

We do not turn on Google's optional logging. We do not upload your paper to Google's file storage.

You can read Google's terms at ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms.

5. What our server keeps

Our server does not keep your papers or photos. It keeps short technical notes, like the kind of paper and how long it took. It deletes them after 30 days. The notes never hold your paper's words, what you type, or the AI's words.

Our server reads each request in its memory and lets go of it when the answer is sent. It writes no copy of your paper to disk.

It keeps a few files that hold no one's papers: the state of our AI keys, the words of the app in each language, and counts of the feedback buttons.

Technical logs list things like the time, which part of the service was used, how long it took, the kind of paper (for example "medical bill"), how urgent it looked, the language, and the scrambled device code. They never hold your photo or the words of your paper. We keep technical logs for 30 days, then only totals.

Connection logs list your internet address (IP address) and which page of our service was asked for. We keep them for 7 days.

We cannot see, retrieve or delete your paper because we do not have it.

6. Your settings that travel with a paper

Your language, your state, your ZIP prefix and your calendar notes (like Ramadan) are sent with each paper so the answer fits you. They are not kept on our server.

We never ask about your immigration status. We do not ask for your exact address. Only your state and the first 3 digits of your ZIP code, if you choose to give them.

7. The random device code

Your phone sends a random code so one phone cannot use the service too much. The code is scrambled and forgotten after 30 days. You can reset it with 'Delete everything'.

The code is made by the app. It is not your phone number or any ID from your phone maker.

8. What stays on your phone or in your browser

Papers you choose to save stay on this phone, protected by your phone's own encryption. Turn on a phone passcode.

In this browser, papers are kept only while the tab is open, unless you turn on 'Keep on this device'. The app does not encrypt them.

You can black out parts of a page before it is read.

You can delete one paper, one family member's papers, or everything, any time.

Reminders store only a label and a date. The reminder text is generic unless you choose otherwise.

No paper is saved unless you tap Save.

9. Feedback

When you tap 'Was this clear?' or 'Report a problem', we count it. We do not receive the text of your paper.

10. No ads, no sale, no tracking

We do not sell your information. There are no ads and no trackers.

We do not use analytics or advertising software.

11. Children

This app is for adults 18 and older. It is not directed at children.

When the app explains a school paper, it explains it to the parent or guardian.

12. Your choices and rights

  • Stop sending papers: in Settings, tap "Stop sending papers". The app will not send any more papers until you agree again.
  • Delete: you can delete one paper, one person's papers, or everything. "Delete everything on this phone" also resets the random device code. In a browser, use "Delete everything in this browser".
  • New device code: in Settings, tap "New device id".

Some state laws give you the right to see, fix or delete personal data a company holds about you. We hold no copy of your papers, your questions or the answers, so there is nothing for us to show, fix or delete. The papers you saved are on your own device, and you can delete them there.

13. Security

The app talks to our server only over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).

Our AI keys and other secrets are kept in a secrets manager, never in the app.

Papers you save stay on this phone, protected by your phone's own encryption. Turn on a phone passcode.

Papers you keep stay in this browser. The app does not encrypt them.

14. If something goes wrong

If your paper is ever exposed in a way you did not agree to, we will post a notice here and in the app within 60 days and tell you what happened.

15. Laws that apply

HIPAA does not apply to this app because we do not work for your doctor, hospital or insurer.

We follow the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule: if health information is exposed without your consent, we will tell you and the FTC, as that rule requires.

State privacy laws may also apply, such as Washington's My Health My Data Act, the California Consumer Privacy Act and the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act.

16. Changes

When this policy changes, the app shows the new consent screen before sending another paper.
